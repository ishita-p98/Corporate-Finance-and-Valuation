# Corporate Finance & Valuation

## Can market timing decisions shape a firm's capital structure for decades?

A collaborative academic research case study of **Baker and Wurgler's _Market Timing and Capital Structure_**, prepared for the *Corporate Finance & Valuation* course.

The project explores how firms' past equity-financing decisions may leave lasting effects on leverage, and what that means for traditional trade-off and pecking-order explanations of capital structure.

### Main takeaway

The paper argues that **capital structure reflects a firm's financing history**, not only its current characteristics or a continuously rebalanced optimal leverage target. The group presentation highlights the persistence of historical market-valuation effects, including a reported **73% remaining book-leverage effect after ten years**.

> **Scope:** This is a research-paper presentation adapted into a written case study. The empirical findings and methods belong to Baker and Wurgler; the group did not independently replicate the study or develop a new dataset.
